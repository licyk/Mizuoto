Mizuoto's own speech recognition: Whisper large-v3-turbo ([openai/whisper](https://github.com/openai/whisper); weights MIT), used for the transcripts of reference clips and voices, for dataset preparation and for the contract suite's word error rate. Code: `mizuoto/engines/asr`. It is not offered for synthesis. It reproduces exactly what Mizuoto 0.1 ran, transformers 4.57.3's `pipeline("automatic-speech-recognition")`, and gives 0.1's transcripts token for token, in about half the time on a CPU. User-facing details: [Engines in depth](/docs/advanced-engines#speech-recognition-asr).

## Variant and files

One variant, `whisper` (catalogue model `asr/whisper-large-v3-turbo`); the model is built from `config.json`, so other Whisper sizes in the same format load too. The folder holds `config.json`, `generation_config.json`, `model.safetensors` (or `pytorch_model.bin`), `preprocessor_config.json`, `tokenizer.json` and `tokenizer_config.json`.

The engine answers one operation, `call("transcribe", {"path", "language", "options"})`, which returns `{text, language, segments, duration}`. `AsrService` holds one lock for it, passes the setting `tts.asr_beams` (default 5) as `beam_size`, and caches transcripts by the audio's SHA-256, the model and that width.

Precision: bf16 on a CUDA GPU that has it (else fp16), fp32 elsewhere; `engines.asr.dtype` overrides.

## How it works

0.1 called the pipeline with `chunk_length_s=30, batch_size=4, return_timestamps=True` and the task and language as generation arguments. The engine does the same:

1. The WAV is read as soundfile reads it (channels averaged) and resampled to 16 kHz by soxr (its HQ default).
2. Without a language, the language is detected on the first 30 s (one decoder step over the language tokens).
3. The audio is cut into 30 s windows overlapping by 5 s on each side, each window's features computed as `WhisperFeatureExtractor` computes them, four windows per `generate` call.
4. `generate` decodes each window with timestamps, by beam search with 5 beams, running Whisper's seek loop inside the window.
5. The windows' tokens are merged into text and timestamped segments by the pipeline's `_decode_asr`.

| Module | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `engine.py` | 0.1's pipeline: reading, resampling, language detection, windows, `generate`, merging |
| `model.py` | `WhisperForConditionalGeneration` as transformers 4.57.3 computes it (the SDPA path, the encoder's clamping, the cross-attention cache) |
| `decoding.py` | Generation settings, the logits processors in transformers' order, the vectorised beam search, greedy decoding, the seek loop |
| `text.py` | Decoding as `WhisperTokenizerFast`, language ids, window bounds, `decode_asr` |

Shared: `nn/dsp/mel.py` (`hf_whisper_filters`, `hf_whisper_log_mel`), `nn/dsp/wav.py`.

## What reproduces upstream

- **Five beams.** The pipeline's default generation config (`num_beams=5`) wins over the model's greedy default, so 0.1 always decoded with 5 beams, whatever `beam_size` said; the option `beam_size` 1 keeps those 5 beams, and a larger value sets the number. 0.1 never passed the option at all; Mizuoto passes `tts.asr_beams`, whose default 5 gives 0.1's transcripts. The beam search is transformers 4.57's vectorised `_beam_search` (log-softmax, then the processors, `2 × beams` candidates).
- **The seek loop inside each window.** Every window is padded to 30 s; a decoding that ends on a timestamp pair is decoded again from that timestamp (the features shifted, zeros after), and the tokens after the pair are dropped. `_decode_asr` re-bases the restarted timestamps and merges the overlapping windows.
- **Features** are transformers' `WhisperFeatureExtractor`, with its own float64 slaney filterbank and a transposed product, not openai-whisper's log-mel. Turbo's `tokenizer_config.json` sets `clean_up_tokenization_spaces` (" ." becomes "."), applied per segment.
- **Attention**: the query projected then scaled by `head_dim ** -0.5`, SDPA with `scale=1` on contiguous q, k and v; the decoder causal without a mask for a multi-token prompt and unmasked per new token, its cache grown by concatenation.

## Deviations

- **The model is its own.** CosyVoice's speech tokenizers also contain a Whisper encoder, but openai's attention there (q and k each scaled by d^-¼, masked) is another computation, so the two are not shared.
- **Exact shortcuts**: language detection's encoder output is reused when there is a single window, and the cross-attention cache is not reordered when every beam's rows are bit-identical. Neither changes a token.

## Memory

One phase per transcription: the whole model, the decoder marked `hot` (it runs per token and beam), `kind="sequence"`. With `compute.oom_fallback`, a transcription that runs out of memory decodes one 30 s window at a time instead of four; that is another batch shape, so in rare ties a transcript may differ.

## Parity

0.1's transcripts exactly on the CPU in fp32: text, segments, language and every window's tokens on 57 corpus clips (language detected or given) and 6 clips of 50–124 s; at module level also the features and `_decode_asr` (3000 fuzzed windows). The stack's parity cases that transcribe their reference (a Qwen3-TTS clone and a CosyVoice 2 clone) are identical as well, so the contract suite's word error rates are unchanged.

Through the stack, `scripts/parity/cases/asr.jsonl` (seven clips, 4–120 s, English, Chinese and mixed, language detected or given) gives 0.1's transcripts exactly on the CPU in fp32 and on a GPU (an RTX 3090, 2026-10-19): language, text and every segment's timestamps.

## Paraformer (Chinese)

The `paraformer` variant (catalogue model `asr/paraformer-zh`, weights Apache-2.0) is FunASR's `AutoModel(model=paraformer-large, vad_model=fsmn-vad, punc_model=ct-punc).generate(input=path)`, the "达摩 ASR" GPT-SoVITS's `tools/asr/funasr_asr.py` uses for Chinese: Mandarin with English words, punctuated, numbers in characters. Code: `mizuoto/engines/asr/paraformer`, ported from FunASR 1.4.21 (MIT). `tts.asr_model_zh = "asr/paraformer-zh"` makes it the recogniser for clips whose language is Chinese once it is downloaded (`AsrService.model_for`); Whisper keeps every other language.

**Files.** The model's folder is the recogniser's ModelScope repository (`model.pt`, `config.yaml`, `am.mvn`, `tokens.json`), with the VAD's in `vad/` and the punctuation model's in `punc/`, each pinned to its commit; all three are on the `mizuoto-model` mirror. `AsrEngine` tells the variants apart by the folder (`config.yaml` naming `Paraformer`, `vad/` and `punc/` beside it).

| Module | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `paraformer/engine.py` | `inference_with_vad` as it runs on the CPU: reading, resampling, VAD, a segment at a time, joining, punctuation |
| `paraformer/model.py` | `WavFrontend` (Kaldi fbank, LFR 7/6, CMVN), `SANMEncoder` (50 layers), `CifPredictorV2`, `ParaformerSANMDecoder`, greedy decoding |
| `paraformer/sanm.py` | SAN-M layers (attention with an FSMN memory block), shared by the recogniser and the punctuation model |
| `paraformer/vad.py` | FSMN-VAD: `WavFrontendOnline`, the FSMN, and the streaming state machine as file inference drives it |
| `paraformer/punc.py` | CT-Transformer: word splitting, mini-sentences with their carry-over, the punctuation rules |
| `paraformer/text.py` | `sentence_postprocess` (tokens to text, English words, abbreviations) and `_join_vad_texts` |

**What reproduces upstream.**
- The clip read as `torchaudio.load` reads it and resampled by `torchaudio.transforms.Resample` (`nn/dsp/resample.Resample`).
- FSMN-VAD's segments as FunASR 1.4.21 makes them: 60 s chunks with the frontend's held-back samples, the LFR context, the FSMN's memory and the state machine carried across them, and the dynamic silence schedule that replaces the config's end silence (2 s, then 0.2 s after a chunk boundary inside speech) and speech threshold (0.5); speech longer than 60 s cut there.
- Each segment recognised alone, shortest first, as FunASR does on a CPU (`batch_size = 0`); its features with `dither` 1.0 (Paraformer's frontend default).
- The randomness: upstream draws the dither from torch's global generator, and its layer stacks (`MultiSequential`) draw one uniform number per layer at every forward for a layer drop that does nothing at inference. Both move the generator the next segment's dither draws from; the port draws both from one generator in the same order.
- Text: special tokens dropped, English sub-words joined, runs of single letters made one upper-case word; the segments' texts joined without a space between Chinese characters; CT-Transformer over 20-word mini-sentences, carrying the words after each mini-sentence's last stop into the next, and its rules (a space before an ASCII word at a mini-sentence's start, capitalisation after a full stop, a final `。` added).

**Deviations.**
- **The dither's generator is seeded 0 for each transcription.** Upstream's global generator stands wherever its process left it (FunASR seeds it once while building the models, which then draw from it), so upstream transcribes the same clip differently from call to call. Here a transcript is upstream's when its generator stands at seed 0 before `generate`, and the same every time (transcripts are cached by the audio).
- **On a GPU a segment at a time too.** Upstream batches segments of up to 300 s on a GPU, padded, which changes its results; here the GPU runs the CPU's order. Results on a GPU are not bit-identical to the CPU's (on an RTX 3060, 14 of 16 clips identical, two noisy synthetic ones a few characters apart).

**Memory.** Three handles, one phase each: the VAD (2 MB) over the clip, the recogniser (880 MB) per segment, the punctuation model (290 MB, mostly its 272,727-word embedding) over the text; float32 on every device. On an RTX 3060 a transcription peaked at 1.1 GB of torch allocations (the catalogue's `vram_mb`, 1,200).

**Parity.** Against FunASR 1.4.21 on the CPU in fp32 (8 threads), on 16 clips (12 of 3–7 s from eight engines, four of 60–124 s, two of them mixed Chinese and English): every transcript and VAD segment identical, and at module level the features, encoder, predictor, decoder, the VAD's scores per chunk (also 11 edge cases: silence, noise, clips under 0.5 s, exact and near multiples of 60 s) and the punctuation model's logits (32 texts, mini-sentence carry-over included) bit-identical; the text post-processing on 14,000 fuzzed token lists. The workspace is `experiment/paraformer`.

## Limitations

- Speech recognition is used inside Mizuoto, not offered as a synthesis model or through `GET /v1/models`.
- The out-of-memory fallback can change a transcript in rare ties.
