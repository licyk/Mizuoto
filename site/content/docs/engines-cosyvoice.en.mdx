Alibaba Tongyi's LLM + flow-matching TTS ([QwenAudio/CosyVoice](https://github.com/QwenAudio/CosyVoice), Apache-2.0). Code: `mizuoto/engines/cosyvoice`. Given the same voice prompt it is identical to upstream (on torch 2.8) sample for sample on the CPU, for every model and mode; through the whole stack, where each side makes its own voice prompt, it agrees to 23–68 dB SNR, for reasons explained below. Its trainers give upstream's checkpoints byte for byte. User-facing details: [Engines in depth](/docs/advanced-engines#cosyvoice).

## Variants and files

| Variant | Catalogue model | Rate | Language model | Flow | Vocoder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `v1_base` | `cosyvoice/cosyvoice-300m` | 22.05 kHz | `TransformerLM`: Conformer text encoder + transformer, Whisper's vocabulary | `MaskedDiffWithXvec`: Conformer, length regulator, Matcha U-Net | HiFT |
| `v1_sft` | `cosyvoice/cosyvoice-300m-sft` | 22.05 kHz | as `v1_base`, with preset speakers | as `v1_base` | HiFT |
| `v1_instruct` | `cosyvoice/cosyvoice-300m-instruct` | 22.05 kHz | as `v1_base`, with instructions | as `v1_base` | HiFT |
| `v2` | `cosyvoice/cosyvoice2-0.5b` | 24 kHz | `Qwen2LM` on Qwen2-0.5B | `CausalMaskedDiffWithXvec`: causal U-Net | HiFT |
| `v3` | `cosyvoice/cosyvoice3-0.5b` | 24 kHz | `CosyVoice3LM` | `CausalMaskedDiffWithDiT`: a 22-layer DiT | causal HiFT with a float64 F0 predictor |

The variant comes from the yaml in the folder (`cosyvoice.yaml`, `cosyvoice2.yaml`, `cosyvoice3.yaml`); a CosyVoice 1 folder with `spk2info.pt` is SFT or Instruct. The folder holds the yaml, `llm.pt`, `flow.pt`, `hift.pt`, `campplus.onnx`, `speech_tokenizer_v1.onnx` / `v2` / `v3`, `spk2info.pt`, and for v2/v3 the `CosyVoice-BlankEN` config and tokenizer (not its weights, which `llm.pt` replaces). The shared asset `cosyvoice/wetext` holds the text normaliser's FSTs. The ONNX models are converted once and cached as `<model>/.mizuoto/converted-<key>.safetensors` (a `<name>.safetensors` beside the ONNX file wins).

Capabilities: preset speakers, instructions, cloning and cross-lingual cloning, voice conversion, streamed audio, and for v2/v3 streamed text input. Precision: fp32; the load option `fp16` runs the language model and the flow under CUDA autocast, as upstream's `fp16` does.

## How it works

The voice prompt is made from the reference three times over: 16 kHz audio to a Whisper log-mel to the **speech tokenizer**'s tokens; 16 kHz audio to a kaldi fbank to **CAM++**'s 192-dim speaker embedding; and always a 24 kHz Matcha mel, even for the 22.05 kHz CosyVoice 1. The **language model** turns text (and the prompt's text and tokens) into speech tokens; the **flow** turns tokens into a mel in 10 Euler steps with guidance 0.7; **HiFT** (a HiFi-GAN with a neural source filter and an inverse STFT) turns the mel into audio.

| Module | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `engine.py` | Frontend, `CosyVoiceModel.tts` (tokens, mel, waveform), streaming chunks, voice conversion, streamed text, voice caches |
| `config.py` | The HyperPyYAML file read as data (`!new:`/`!name:` as dicts, `!ref` expressions resolved) |
| `frontend.py` | `text_normalize` with wetext, cleaning and splitting |
| `tokenizer.py` | v2/v3: BlankEN's Qwen2 BPE with CosyVoice's added tokens; v1: Whisper's tiktoken vocabulary |
| `llm.py` | The three language models, repetition-aware sampling (RAS), the streamed-text interleave (5 text : 15 speech) |
| `flow.py`, `dit.py` | The three flows; CosyVoice 3's DiT estimator |
| `train.py`, `sft.py` | `CosyVoiceTrainer` (methods `llm`, `llm_flow`) and its maths |

Shared: `nn/codecs/s3tokenizer.py` (speech tokenizers v1–v3 from their ONNX files), `nn/speaker/campplus.py` (CAM++ as the ONNX graph has it), `nn/transformer/wenet.py`, `nn/flow/unet1d.py`, `nn/transformer/dit.py`, `nn/vocoders/hift.py`, `nn/dsp/` (`mel`, `kaldi`, `resample`, `wav`, `flac`), `runtime/convert/onnx.py`, `runtime/lm/qwen.py`, `runtime/graphs.py`, `text/normalize/wetext.py`, `text/tokenize/`.

## What reproduces upstream

- **Sizes from the yaml.** Module sizes come from the model's own yaml (HyperPyYAML's tags read as data), not from code.
- **Speech tokens and CAM++ without onnxruntime.** The speech tokenizers are s3tokenizer's torch models with the weights read from the ONNX file; their codes equal onnxruntime's on the test corpus for v1, v2 and v3. CAM++ mirrors the exported graph (batch norms after convolutions folded, as the export folded them) and agrees with onnxruntime to about 1e-6 relative, not bit for bit.
- **The prompt mel** is Matcha's at 24 kHz for every version; v2/v3 keep exactly two mel frames per prompt token. v3 drops runs of more than five silent tokens and gets its system prompt (`You are a helpful assistant.<|endofprompt|>`).
- **Qwen2 as transformers 4.51 ran it.** CosyVoice 2/3's language model ran on transformers 4.51, whose SDPA path always repeats the KV heads and makes q, k and v contiguous (`QwenConfig.sdpa_gqa=False`).
- **Noise.** v2/v3's flow noise is upstream's seed-0 buffer (the first 1×80×15000 normal draws). `randn_like(x)` keeps `x`'s strides, so its draws land in another element order than `randn(shape)` when `x` is transposed (HiFT's sine source): `nn.vocoders.hift.randn_like` fills `empty_like(x)` from the generator.
- **CosyVoice 3's vocoder noise** is drawn once when upstream builds the model, after the flow's seed-0 noise and the default initialisations of the flow and the F0 predictor. `cosyvoice3_noise` replays those draws on a private generator, so the values are upstream's and the 259 MB buffer is never materialised. Its F0 predictor runs in float64, so its weight norm is folded in float64.
- **Draw order.** Unstreamed, the draws come in upstream's order (language model, v1's flow noise, vocoder noise). Streaming uses upstream's chunking: v1 100 tokens with 20 of overlap and Hamming cross-fades; v2/v3 25 tokens doubling up to 100, with three tokens of lookahead.
- **Text normalisation** is wetext's normaliser ported over kaldifst, identical on the corpus; its FSTs are the asset `cosyvoice/wetext` (ModelScope `pengzhendong/wetext` at a pinned commit). Text containing `<|…|>` is not normalised, as upstream skips it.

## Deviations

- **No language-model thread.** Upstream runs the language model on a thread beside the vocoder; here it runs in the request's thread and chunks are made as its tokens arrive. Upstream's streamed v1/v2 draw vocoder noise on the main thread while the language model samples on its own, so their streamed audio depends on timing; Mizuoto's vocoder draws from its own generator (seed + 1) and the tokens are those of the unstreamed request. CosyVoice 3, whose noise is fixed, streams identically.
- **Normalisation that 0.1 lacked.** Upstream's frontend downloads the FSTs at run time; in 0.1's worker that download failed and upstream silently skipped normalisation. With the FSTs an asset, Chinese text with digits now has its numbers read out, so such text sounds different from 0.1.
- **On a device without float64** (Apple MPS, Intel Arc), CosyVoice 3's F0 predictor stays on the CPU while the rest of the model moves; the vocoder feeds it on its own device.
- **TensorRT, vLLM and JIT** (0.1's load options `load_trt`, `trt_concurrent`, `load_vllm`) are not available.
- Kept from upstream: in streamed-text mode with a long reference (7 s) the speech can begin with the reference's last words; plain synthesis with the same reference does not. Streamed text needs the reference's transcript.

## Why the whole stack is not bit-identical

Through the stack each side computes its own voice prompt, and CAM++'s embedding is the one part not bit-exact: it differs from onnxruntime's by at most 7e-6 (cosine 0.999999999997). The flow turns that into a mel change of at most 2.7e-3, and HiFT's sine source integrates the F0 into phase (a float32 cumulative sum over the whole utterance, nine harmonics), so the error grows along the utterance: on a CosyVoice 3 English clone the SNR per half second falls from 66 to 20 dB. Upstream is just as sensitive. Its own vocoder chain fed Mizuoto's embedding gives exactly Mizuoto's audio; its own onnxruntime CAM++ with 8 threads instead of 1 gives 31 dB; random perturbations of that size give 20–33 dB. CosyVoice 2/3's language model never uses the speaker embedding, so the speech tokens are the same.

## Memory and speed

Phases: the frontend (speech tokenizer and CAM++) only for a voice that is not cached and for conversion; the language model (`kind="sequence"`) makes the tokens; the flow and HiFT speak them. Streaming holds the language model and the flow together. HiFT's F0 predictor is marked `fixed` (HiFT reads its device). The language model's working memory is its cache for upstream's bound (20 speech tokens a text token after a 300-position prompt), at the KV width the model has (Qwen2's two KV heads), with one layer's copy repeated per head group, plus 512 MB. `compute.kv_cache = static` gives the Qwen2 model preallocated caches (identical on the CPU).

**Decoding on CUDA (CosyVoice 2/3).** Upstream's samplers run a Python loop over the sorted probabilities, reading the running sum back at each kept token, copying probabilities to the host and back and checking the repetition window on the host: some 50–80 waits for the device per token, on top of 24 layers launched eagerly. On CUDA `llm._Steps` draws the same tokens with one wait per token: the nucleus loop is replayed on the device over the first 25 sorted tokens (the same 0-d additions in the scores' dtype; the draw is `multinomial`'s own `argmax(p / Exp(1))`, whose first *n* exponentials are those an *n*-element draw uses), the same token and generator state as upstream's loop for every *n* from 1 to 25, in fp32, fp16 and bf16. The resample after a repetition stays eager. With `compute.cuda_graphs` (NVIDIA, the language model wholly on its device) the one-token steps run as graphs around their attention calls and the draw as one graph; the graphs' generator is synced with the request's at each token, since CosyVoice 3's streamed flow draws from it between tokens. The real CosyVoice 2 model gives the same tokens with and without graphs, about seven times faster per token. Other devices keep upstream's loop.

`compute.compile` wraps the flow estimator. On the CPU it gains nothing (the U-Net's convolutions and products are already library kernels); the mel stays within 5.7e-6 of eager, which HiFT's F0-driven source amplifies to about 1e-2 in the audio (47 dB), as eager with another thread count also does.

## Training

Methods `llm` and `llm_flow` (stages `features`, `llm`, `flow`, `export`; from CosyVoice 1, 2 or 3): upstream's LibriTTS recipe and `bin/train.py`, without onnxruntime, pyarrow, pyworld, transformers, deepspeed or torchrun. It gives upstream's losses, accuracies, learning rates and `epoch_<N>_whole.pt` byte for byte on the CPU (gloo, world size 1), for all three generations, with zero or two loader workers and an accumulation left over at an epoch's end; the features (speech tokens and CAM++) give onnxruntime's tokens and its embeddings within 6e-6. Upstream trains on CUDA only; the trainer runs on every device kind.

- **The reference** is upstream with its generators seeded with the stage's seed once its model is built: upstream seeds 1986 in its yaml (its v2/v3 flow reseeds 0 while it is built) and then draws its random initialisation, while nothing draws here before training.
- **Data loading.** Each loader worker runs the whole pipeline on its shards; with fewer shards (1000 clips each) than workers every worker reads every shard, so with the default two workers each clip is seen twice an epoch, as upstream. Shuffles draw from `Random(base_seed + k)`; batches come round-robin; features are made on one thread, as in a worker.
- **A validation pass** on the training list ends each epoch, as in upstream, and it draws too (Qwen2LM's bistream choice, the flow's streaming choice, conditions and noise). Its losses are logged, not metrics.
- **Clipping order.** Parameters are clipped in upstream's order (`sft._ORDER`), since the total norm is summed in it; checkpoints keep upstream's key order, with Qwen's tied `lm_head` sharing the embedding's storage.
- **Qwen2 trains under transformers 4.51's additive mask** (`causal_mask_451`); the logged `loss` is divided by the accumulation steps, as upstream logs it; CosyVoice 1 keeps the recipe's `WarmupLR` (2500 steps).
- 0.1 could not train CosyVoice 2 or 3 (upstream asks for a `speech_tokenizer_v*.batch.onnx` no model ships). Mizuoto's export loads in both implementations and speaks identically in both given the same voice prompt.
- `training.memory`: gradient checkpointing over the language model's or the flow's blocks gives identical losses and weights.

## Parity

The reference is upstream on torch 2.8 (on its own torch 2.3.1 it disagrees with itself on 2.8). With upstream's voice prompt fed to both, cloning (English, Chinese with numbers), cross-lingual cloning, instructions, preset speakers, streamed audio and voice conversion are identical sample for sample for v1, v2 and v3. Through the stack, each side making its own prompt:

- most v2 cases: 45.5–68.3 dB SNR;
- the CosyVoice 3 English clone: about 23 dB (CAM++'s float noise, above);
- the SFT Chinese case and the v2 Chinese clone differ in length, from normalisation; with normalisation off on both sides the first is identical and the second differs only by CAM++'s noise;
- the training cross-load cases (one implementation's export spoken by the other): 57.4 and 38.1 dB.

The contract suite passes (18 tests).

## Limitations

- Through the whole stack, results agree closely but not bit for bit with upstream, because CAM++ is not bit-exact with onnxruntime.
- Streamed CosyVoice 1/2 audio is not upstream's streamed audio, which depends on thread timing; it is reproducible here.
- The fast decoding path is CUDA only.
